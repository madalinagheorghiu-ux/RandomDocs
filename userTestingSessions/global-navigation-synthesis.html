1Global navigation

1.1The global header overall

Nobody struggled with the new arrangement. The redesign's success here is that it went largely unremarked.

Key points
  • It does not announce itself, and that is the point. Asked directly whether anything looked different, one participant said no — then completed every task without difficulty. DeliaAdriana
  • Gathering config and runtime on the left is the most-agreed improvement of the whole redesign. Raised unprompted by two participants; contested by none. ȘtefanAdriana
  • The header is filling up. The open question is not this release but the next one: how many more things can the top bar hold. Cristi D
Strengths
  • Config and runtime on the left keeps the eye in one zone. No more crossing to the right and back — and no more opening a menu without realising you are in the wrong mode. AdrianaȘtefan
  • Overall more intuitive and better organised. Summary verdict after the first block of tasks. Ștefan
  • The notification centre is visible and straightforward. Opened unprompted in the first minutes. Adriana
  • Moving the AI entry point into the header is right. A floating bubble that blocks the screen is unwanted — people who want to build with AI will go to it without being pushed. Cristi D
  • Being able to leave comments answers an existing need. The current workaround is writing notes into a script and commenting them out. Delia
Weak points
  • The top bar is running out of room. Archiving unused branches will help the switcher, but the trend needs another answer before long. Cristi D

1.2Organisation settings — getting there, and getting back

The weakest area of the redesign, and the one where participants disagree about whether the underlying problem is real.

Key points
  • Discoverability under the account avatar is the single biggest gap. Two of four found it only by reasoning or searching; one says she would never have found it at all. ȘtefanDeliaAdriana
  • All four expect "back" to return them to the exact place they left — which is the intended behaviour. The prototype's bug sent them to a default workspace. all four
  • The word "back" does not say where. Processes, projects, or where you actually were are all plausible readings, and the tooltip contradicts the behaviour. Cristi D
  • Whether context loss is worth fixing at all is contested — see the decision box below.
Strengths
  • Back is the easiest way out once found. Press it and you are straight where you need to be — the route participants reached for over every alternative. AdrianaDelia
  • A moved entry point did not block anyone. One reasoned his way there — anything about users and organisations would sit under my own avatar — and another found it by searching. ȘtefanDelia
  • Expectations match the design intent. Return to the workspace you were in, not a default one; only one narrow exception was offered, and it was self-identified. AdrianaDeliaȘtefan
Weak points
  • "I would never have looked there." The account icon holds what belongs to you — your password, your email — not what belongs to the organisation. A cog beside the profile, not settings inside it. Adriana
  • Organisation has left the hierarchy. The mental model is organisation above workspace above projects; reaching it from the account menu makes it feel like it sits on a different scale entirely. Cristi D
  • "Go back" is ambiguous, and the label contradicts the behaviour. The tooltip reads go back to workspace while the behaviour returns you to the exact spot you left. Cristi D
  • Without a back control, one participant did not know how to return at all. Delia
  • The real navigation frustration may be elsewhere entirely. Asked whether leaving organisation settings creates friction, the answer was no — followed immediately by what does: being logged out mid-work and having to walk the whole path again. Adriana

On the table: organisation as a third tab

If organisation settings is simply another context — as switching between config and runtime already is — then give it a tab. Press organization and you go there; press config and you come back. One move that answers both the placement question and the go-back ambiguity. — proposed by Cristi D, who flagged himself that product may object.

2The bottom bar

2.1Versioning & branching — usage and expectations

The move to the bottom is accepted by everyone who commented on it. The disagreement is about ordering, not placement.

Key points
  • The placement is settled: nobody objected. One called it intuitive and easy; another saw no downside beyond it not being where you expect. AdrianaCristi D
  • Discoverability rests on colour, not on position. One participant found the controls because they are coloured and said she would not have spotted them as quickly otherwise; another never realised the bar was clickable at all. AdrianaDelia
  • Quick access and the full console are wanted as complements, not alternatives. The compact one for switching, the console when you need to see what changed. ȘtefanAdriana
  • Landing on the latest version after a branch switch is confirmed correct by everyone asked. AdrianaDelia
Strengths
  • Intuitive and easy to navigate. A warm, repeated verdict on the bottom bar as a whole. Adriana
  • The move frees the header. Room for the run button — so you no longer go back to run — and somewhere to put notifications. Cristi D
  • Two routes to switch version is the right call. Compact by default because there is less to get lost in; the console when you also want the changed resources. Ștefan
  • The console closing on switch is a feature, not a flaw. The close is the signal that the switch happened — no separate confirmation needed. Adriana
Weak points
  • Branch rows show the creator, not the last editor. The person who touched it last is the person you go to — a colleague lost time that same day double-checking a build for exactly this reason. Ștefan
  • The bar does not look clickable. One participant went in through the branching icon every time, never considering the bar was a control. Delia
  • Select-then-switch is a step more than expected. Selecting, switching, then closing is three actions where there are two today. Adriana
  • No current-version indicator on the page. Asked to change version, she first had to work out where she already was. Adriana
  • Export from the version console gives a version, not a build. The filename changed to a date, which read as a new naming convention rather than a warning. Ștefan
  • A personalised branch list would cost more than it gives. Shared order is what lets you sit next to a colleague and look at the same thing. Adriana
⚡ Unresolved — branch ordering What should the quick-access list be ordered by?
Most recently changed, everywhere. Knowing the first entry is always the most recent means navigating without reading. — Ștefan
Creation date, because it does not move. With eleven branches on one project, ordering by modification makes the list a constant ping-pong; creation date is stable and the eye can learn it. — Adriana

2.2Issues console — usage and expectations

The most strongly validated part of the release. The open questions are when and where issues appear, not whether the console is wanted.

Key points
  • The floating console beat what participants predicted. Two independently expected something else — a new tab, a classic dialog — and preferred what they got. ȘtefanDelia
  • It is strong enough to become a team standard. One participant would write zero warnings and zero errors into his team's definition of done. Cristi D
  • Everyone wants to control how much screen it takes. Dock it, split it, shrink it to a strip — rather than the platform deciding half or full. Cristi DDeliaȘtefan
  • "In context" is the weakest label in the release. One could not form any guess at what it does; two others reached the right reading only after several passes. ȘtefanDeliaCristi D
Strengths
  • It surfaces what is only discoverable at runtime today. A swimlane with no role assigned currently shows up when you reach it and it fails. Cristi D
  • A floating, movable panel beats both alternatives participants imagined. It survives moving between menus and keeps the surrounding information visible. ȘtefanDeliaCristi D
  • Clicking an issue navigates to the node, as predicted. The highlight-in-place versus navigate-on-link split matched expectations. Cristi DȘtefan
  • Both groupings read correctly without prompting — by resource type and by severity, with what each is useful for. Cristi DDelia
  • Click-to-toggle severity chips beat a dropdown. Ștefan
  • Almost-real-time refresh is good enough. Thirty seconds to a minute of delay is not a problem — a constraint removed rather than added. Cristi D
Weak points
  • Clicking an issue hides the result behind the panel. The navigation works, but you cannot see it happen. Wanted: the panel drops into a bottom tab like a chat window, and the affected node blinks. Ștefan
  • No way to search by process or workflow. With thirty workflows in a project, scrolling to the one you care about is slow. ȘtefanDelia
  • Severity cannot be set by the user. Deliberate choices keep presenting as errors, taking up visual space and dulling attention to the ones that matter. Delia
  • Scope is too narrow. Misconfiguration should cover data sources too — a REST source that fails to return 200 when tested is equally a misconfiguration. Cristi D
  • Grouping by resource is unclear, where grouping by severity is understood immediately. Ștefan
  • The issue list inside a node panel sits awkwardly, pushed to the bottom. Cristi D
⚡ Unresolved — should issue markers be on by default? Three positions, each sound from its own premise.
On. Anything you have to switch on may never be discovered — you do not know it exists and were never curious. — Ștefan, who triages failures
Off. A process that is only just started always carries issues — a missing end node, leftover nodes producing unreachable-node errors. Worse, dead nodes are often left deliberately, like commented-out code, to compare old behaviour against new. The platform would be flagging her own decisions. — Delia, who configures solutions
Split the surfaces. Populate the console from the very start — a beginner benefits from being told what is missing, and it deflects questions from the support team. But markers spread across every resource only once you signal you are done: a whole flow turning red is discouraging and pushes people into fixing node by node, generating work that later proves unnecessary. — Cristi D

3Decisions to make

  1. Issue marker default — on, off, or after a milestone. Cristi's split (console from the start, markers after) reconciles the other two and is the only proposal that accounts for both premises.
  2. Branch ordering — creation date or last modified. Genuinely contested; both reason from real projects. Worth deciding deliberately rather than by whoever was asked last.
  3. Organisation settings placement — keep it under the account avatar, add a cog beside the profile, or make it a third tab. Two of four participants object to the current placement on mental-model grounds, not on discoverability alone.
  4. Whether to invest in context preservation — one participant has never hit the problem, solves it by duplicating a browser tab, and would not prioritise it. Another's real frustration is session expiry, which this work does not touch.
  5. "In context" naming — the weakest label tested. It is decipherable but not self-evident to anyone.

One finding that belongs to no section

Going back from a process instance throws away your scroll position. Ștefan scrolls back hours to find a failure, opens one, returns — and starts again. Workflow instances does not do this, because it opens a dialog he can leave from. He called fixing it the single largest quality-of-life gain available, and it has nothing to do with the redesign.
